### Activation steering / representation engineering (Turner et al.; Zou et al., 2023)

Take a model that has just politely declined to explain how to assemble something explosive. Without touching a single weight, without retraining, without slipping it a clever prompt — you add to its internal activations at the tenth layer a small vector. And on the very same request the model begins eagerly writing out the instructions. You did not crack it with words — you cracked it with its geometry. And then you add another vector — and it suddenly tells only the truth where before it lied. This is activation steering: a rudder inserted straight into the stream of thought, bypassing both training and text.

The classic demonstration example, from the work on ActAdd, is the "love minus hate vector." You take two short contrasting prompts, "Love" and "Hate," run them through the model, read off the activations at a chosen layer, and subtract one from the other. What you get is a direction in representation space — the "axis of love." Add it with a positive coefficient to the activations during generation on any neutral topic — and the text warms up, becomes about tenderness and closeness. Add it with a negative one — and it cools. By the same recipe one builds an "anger vector," a "weddings vector," a "conspiracy-theories vector." One contrast — one direction — one volume knob.

**The mechanism and its significance.** Under the hood lies the linear representation hypothesis: the idea that abstract features — tone, truthfulness, topic, the intent to refuse — are encoded in the activations approximately linearly, as directions in a high-dimensional space. If so, then a "trait" can be not only *read* by linear probing but also *written*, by shifting the activation along the requisite axis. Representation engineering (RepE), in the work of Zou and co-authors, turns this into a whole "top-down" program: first isolate the direction responsible for a concept (reading), then control by it (control). Their flagship demonstration is the "honesty vector": they find a direction along which the model distinguishes a sincere answer from a lie, and, suppressing or amplifying it, make the model either lie more eagerly or blurt out the unvarnished truth; along the way, using the same direction, one can *detect* that the model is lying, reading its activations as a lie detector.

The value here is twofold, and therein lies the whole point. On one hand — cheap, pinpoint, reversible control: no fine-tuning needed, no labeling of thousands of examples, no expensive RLHF; a couple of contrasting phrases and a single vector addition at inference suffice. On the other — it is a window inward: the very fact that "honesty" or "refusal" lives along a concrete direction is already a claim about how the model is built, checkable and falsifiable. Control and interpretability merge: you understand the model exactly as far as you can steer it, and vice versa.

And the same blade cuts the other way. If a "refusal vector" exists, then it can be not only amplified but also *subtracted* — the ability to say "no" can be suppressed. Arditi and co-authors showed that refusal in open-weights models is mediated by literally a single direction: remove its projection from the activations (ablation) — and the safety restrictions collapse across a broad class of harmful requests, without a single step of fine-tuning. The jailbreak moves from the realm of words into the realm of linear algebra.

**How it came about.** Doing arithmetic with internal vectors is nothing new — as far back as word2vec, with its "king minus man plus woman," we got used to the idea that meanings add up. In 2022 ROME and related knowledge-editing methods (model editing) appeared, showing that concrete facts can be written into the weights through a local intervention. A separate line is Subramani and others, who were extracting "steering vectors" as early as 2022. But in 2023 two collectives, almost simultaneously, shaped the approach into a finished paradigm. Alexander Matt Turner with co-authors (a team that grew out of the alignment-research community) released Activation Addition (ActAdd) — a minimalist, almost bare-bones recipe: contrast, difference, addition. Andy Zou with a large group (including researchers from the Center for AI Safety) rolled out Representation Engineering — an ambitious frame that put activations at the center of AI safety work and treated honesty, emotions, power, morality as readable-and-steerable directions. From there the genre grew instantly: Panickssery and co-authors formalized Contrastive Activation Addition (averaging differences over many pairs), and in 2024 Anthropic, through sparse autoencoders, made the famous Golden Gate Claude — a model with the "Golden Gate Bridge" feature turned up, so that it obsessively steers any conversation to the bridge. By different roads they arrived at the same thing: behavior has handles, and one can reach them.

**Criticism / arguments against.** First and foremost: fragility and side effects. A shift along the "right" axis is almost never clean: representation space is densely packed, the directions are not orthogonal, and, in dialing up "fun," you also break coherence, factual accuracy, or arithmetic. Too weak a coefficient — no effect; too strong — the model collapses into incoherence or loops (that same Golden Gate Claude is, in essence, a caricature of an overdose). There is often no optimal window, and it drifts from layer to layer and from topic to topic.

Second: the incompleteness of the linear hypothesis. Far from everything that matters to us lies along one tidy direction. Some features are nonlinear, distributed, context-dependent; "honesty in general" may turn out to be not a single vector but a family of different mechanisms, glued into one only on average over the dataset. A vector found on one set of contrasts falls apart on another — the generalization is deceptive.

Third, and most insidious: the illusion of control. An elegant demonstration ("look, an honesty vector!") easily gets mistaken for understanding, when in fact it is only a correlation on a narrow distribution. You may have isolated not "truthfulness" but "the style of a confident answer" or an artifact of the prompt format. A model "made honest" by a shift begins, under pressure or on off-distribution input, to weasel again — because you tweaked the indicator rather than removing the cause. This is exactly the trap against which all of interpretability warns: resembling an explanation does not mean being one.

Fourth: misuse. The cheapness and reversibility that make the method convenient for safety are exactly as convenient for attack. For open-weights models, an activation jailbreak — removal of the refusal direction — turns any "aligned" model compliant in a matter of minutes, and no safety-tuning of the weights prevents this, because the intervention happens after the weights, at inference. A defense that lives in a single direction is a defense that can be switched off by a single subtraction.

**What to do about it / where it leads.** The sober view: activation steering is not a panacea for alignment but a powerful research probe and a pinpoint tool. As a means of *reading* it is useful already now — detectors of deception, of hallucinations, of "the model knows that it doesn't know" are built on the same directions and work as a cheap monitor over any request. As a means of *writing* it demands discipline: to measure the collateral damage (not only "became more honest" but also "what broke"), to check generalization outside the training distribution, not to confuse shifting the indicator with actually correcting the behavior.

Strategically, several branches grow out of RepE. Circuit breakers and related approaches (Zou and co-authors, 2024) already use representation control as a built-in defense — not filtering the output but redirecting internal states away from harmful trajectories. Sparse autoencoders promise to replace crude contrastive vectors with a decomposition into interpretable, less entangled features — so as to dial up "honesty" without touching "arithmetic." And the theme of dual use poses an inconvenient but honest question for the whole open-weights policy: if safety restrictions are linearly removable at inference, then "aligned" open weights cannot be considered protected by construction, and one must rely on layers of defense outside the model.

The main thing this line of work gives, though, is a shift in perspective. It made the internal activations not a murky "black box" but a space in which concepts have addresses and behavior has handles. This is at once the most reassuring and the most alarming upshot: if we have learned to find and pull these handles, so will the one who wants to pull in the other direction.
